# Fraud Policy

*JBank — fictional institution, synthetic data only.*

## What Counts as Suspected Fraud

Suspected fraud includes, but is not limited to:
- Transactions the customer does not recognize or did not authorize
- Signs of account takeover (password changes the customer did not make, unfamiliar linked devices)
- Phishing or social engineering attempts reported by the customer
- Suspicious patterns flagged by automated monitoring (e.g., rapid high-value transactions, unusual geographic activity)

## Immediate Response Protocol

Any conversation involving suspected fraud follows a strict protocol:

1. **No transactional actions are taken until identity is verified.** This includes freezing cards, viewing balances, or discussing specific transactions.
2. Once identity is verified, the affected card or account access is frozen immediately to prevent further unauthorized activity.
3. The case is escalated to a human fraud specialist. The automated system does not make final fraud determinations.
4. The customer is given a case number and a summary of next steps, including expected contact timelines from the fraud team (within 1 business day).

## Mandatory Escalation Triggers

The following situations must always be escalated to a human representative, regardless of how the conversation started:
- The customer explicitly states they believe they are a victim of fraud
- The customer reports they shared their credentials with someone else (even accidentally)
- A single transaction or series of transactions exceeds $5,000 and the customer disputes it
- The customer appears to be currently being coerced or scammed in real time (e.g., describing an active phone call instructing them to move money)

## What the Automated System Must Never Do

- Never ask the customer to share a full card number, PIN, CVV, or password in the conversation.
- Never confirm or deny whether a specific account exists before identity verification.
- Never advise a customer to move money to a "safe account" — this is a common scam pattern, and any such instruction should be treated as a red flag requiring immediate escalation, not followed.

## Reimbursement

Reimbursement decisions for confirmed fraud are made by the fraud investigation team, not by the automated system or by front-line representatives. Typical resolution timelines range from 3–10 business days once a case is opened, depending on complexity.
